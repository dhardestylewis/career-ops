## LONG

My career has been organized around one thread: building production ML systems that predict physical-world outcomes for people who have to act on those predictions.

I spent five years at the Texas Advanced Computing Center, UT Austin's supercomputing facility, as a Senior Data Scientist and Technical Lead on federal disaster resilience. The project I'm most proud of there was building real-time flood inundation models for Texas Emergency Management — taking raw outputs from the National Water Model and producing 1-meter resolution flood maps that directly informed evacuation decisions during active storm events. When your model is wrong in that context, people get hurt. That shaped how I think about evaluation, calibration, and operational reliability.

During that same period I competed in DARPA World Modelers — a head-to-head competition with MITRE to build what DARPA called "the Airflow for science," automated pipelines for disaster and food security modeling in East Africa. We were scaling climate simulation workloads across 800,000-node distributed jobs on Frontera, then one of the top five supercomputers in the world. I also partnered directly with the Army Corps of Engineers, NOAA, NSF, and the Texas General Land Office on compound flood hazard modeling — learning to translate between research scientists who want flexibility and government stakeholders who need production reliability on a fixed timeline. My work during this period contributed research toward Paola Passalacqua's 2022 EGU Bagnold Medal, one of geomorphology's highest honors, and received recognition from then-AAAI President Yolanda Gil in her farewell address.

After TACC, I founded Summit Geospatial as a VP of IP spin-out from UT Austin, commercializing the highest-quality seamless terrain data in Texas from the $40M Texas Disaster Information System. I then advised PoliBOM on its agentic trade compliance architecture — a top-5% YC entrant that prompted KPMG to launch a competing product after seeing our demo.

At Homecastr, my current primary focus, I built the entire ML stack from scratch: a nationwide, tract-level diffusion forecasting model with spatial inducing tokens, per-horizon loss normalization, and a probabilistic evaluation suite enforcing temporal leakage prevention before any model ships. The result is a 7% one-year forecast error competitive with Zillow's current off-market accuracy on a strictly harder forward-looking task, and 12.6% MdAE at 4-year horizons on NYC parcel-scale data. I own everything from data ingestion through production serving.

In parallel, I've been doing research at Columbia: a multi-asset CVAE latent factor model that achieves 85% R² against commercial SaaS at 75% and Fama-French at 58%, and GRPO fine-tuning work on reasoning models.

I'm currently a Member Resident at South Park Commons in NYC, in the -1 to 0 phase — exploring spatiotemporal foundation models and causal inference for physical infrastructure decision-support. My core question is whether we can build the foundational data API for the physical world that allows hyperscalers and infrastructure developers to programmatically query the true feasibility and risk of breaking ground on massive new infrastructure.

MS candidate, Urban Planning — Columbia University. BS, Pure Mathematics — UT Austin.

Outside of work: biked Boston to DC, San Diego to San Francisco, and San Francisco to Mendocino. Scaled from V0 to V8 bouldering in a year. Pour-over champion at Lance Armstrong's Austin coffee shop.
